Research Interests

I am primarily interested in building AI systems that can understand visual and multimodal information reliably and remain trustworthy when the data, domain, or deployment setting changes. This has led me to work on computer vision, multimodal and vision-language models, model evaluation, distribution shift and cross-dataset generalization, and uncertainty & calibration. I am also interested in test-time adaptation and low-resource & multilingual AI, particularly in settings where careful benchmarking, abstention, robustness, and systematic failure analysis are as important as raw predictive accuracy.

Completed

Do Open Language Models Invent Scripture? BILAL-BENCH: Benchmarking Islamic-Source Fabrication in Bengali


Accepted — Muslims in ML Workshop at NeurIPS 2026 | First Author
workshop

Introduced BILAL-BENCH, a 240-question expert-verified Bengali benchmark for studying Islamic-source fabrication across Qur'anic attribution, Hadith, Fiqh, contemporary issues, and adversarial false-premise prompts. Evaluated seven open-weight language models from 1B to 120B parameters on 1,680 double-annotated responses using a two-axis rubric that separates source correctness from response assertiveness. The study found persistently high confident fabrication across model scales and especially weak Hadith provenance, highlighting the need for citation verification and better abstention behavior in under-resourced religious QA settings.

Knowing When Not to Answer: Image-Grounded Answerability and Abstention in Medical VQA


Under Review — NAACL 2027 | First Author

Constructed a balanced medical VQA benchmark containing 45,942 answerable and unanswerable image-question pairs across X-ray, MRI, CT, Histology, and Gross Pathology. Developed an answerability-aware framework that converts questions into visual statements, measures image-text alignment with BioMedCLIP, and learns lightweight projections to determine whether the image provides sufficient evidence before answering. The study evaluates both answer correctness and appropriate abstention, with the broader goal of reducing unsupported responses in medical vision-language systems.

Leakage-Controlled Structure-Preserving Augmentation and Template-Aware Evaluation for Bangla Symptom-Based Disease Prediction


Completed Study

Developed a leakage-controlled evaluation pipeline for Bangla symptom-based disease prediction using 758 curated disease-symptom profiles spanning 85 diseases and 172 symptoms. The study applies train-only structure-preserving augmentation after group-aware splitting so that related disease templates remain within the same partition, reducing augmentation and template leakage. Multiple machine-learning models and voting ensembles are evaluated on curated validation/test data, while SHAP and LIME analyses are used to examine which symptoms drive model decisions and whether predictive behavior remains clinically interpretable.

Ongoing

What Does Cross-Dataset Generalization Really Measure? A Dataset-Integrity Study in Brain Tumor MRI


Sabah Nushra
Manuscript in Preparation | Lead Researcher
paper

Conducting a systematic cross-dataset study of CNN, Transformer, and foundation-model architectures across public brain MRI datasets to examine whether strong external performance truly reflects domain generalization. The work combines zero-target-adaptation evaluation with multi-seed analysis, bootstrap uncertainty, dataset-provenance tracing, exact-duplicate auditing, perceptual near-duplicate screening, and within-split leakage checks. The central goal is to quantify how hidden source-target relatedness can inflate or otherwise confound conclusions drawn from nominally independent external benchmarks.

Understanding How Vision Models Fail: A Graph-Based Framework for Severity, Stability, and Model Complementarity


Sabah Nushra
Manuscript in Preparation | Lead Researcher
paper

Developing a graph-based evaluation framework that represents model mistakes as structured class-to-class failure transitions rather than isolated incorrect predictions. The framework introduces Failure Graph Risk (FGR), combining error frequency, prediction confidence, semantic distance, and sample difficulty to characterize the severity of a model's failures. Supporting analyses study how failure structure changes under perturbations and domain shift, and whether complementary failure graphs can help identify model pairs that are more effective for ensembling.

Beyond the Leaderboard: Reliability-Aware Operating-Point Evaluation for Bangla Cyberbullying Moderation


Sabah Nushra
Manuscript in Preparation | Lead Researcher
paper

Evaluating Bangla-specific, multilingual, and compact generative models for five-class cyberbullying moderation under a strictly frozen train-development-test protocol. Rather than relying only on full-coverage accuracy, the study examines a realistic operating point in which the most uncertain comments are deferred for human review, alongside calibration, selective risk, rare-class Threat behavior, realistic Bangla perturbations, subgroup diagnostics, human error analysis, and standardized compute cost. The results show that the system with the best aggregate performance is not necessarily the strongest choice on every safety-critical operating measure.

Enhancing Few-Shot Medical Image Segmentation with Refined Prototypes


Role: Project Lead

Proposed an iterative refinement framework that enhances support and query feature interaction for more accurate segmentation in few-shot medical imaging. Integrated self-attention and cross-masked attention modules to generate contextually enriched feature maps and robust prototype representations. Introduced regional prototype generation to handle intra-class variations and improve adaptability across diverse medical datasets (ABD-CT, ABD-MR, CMR). Achieved superior performance in mean Dice coefficient (DSC), demonstrating improved generalization and segmentation precision over existing methods.
